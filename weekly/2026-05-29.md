## The story of the week

**The repayment restart isn't going as expected.** Three months in, the data shows a borrower base that hasn't recovered. The most recent [NY Fed Household Debt Report](https://www.newyorkfed.org/microeconomics/hhdc) put student loan delinquency at 5.7% — the highest since 2014 — and our Reddit signal shows the language of distress has shifted from "frustrated" to "resigned."

That's the more dangerous reading. Frustrated borrowers call their servicer. Resigned borrowers stop opening mail.

This shift in [borrower behavior on Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/StudentLoans/) is something we've been tracking closely since April. The volume of posts hasn't grown dramatically — but the emotional weight of each post has. AI sentiment analysis (powered by [Anthropic Claude](https://www.anthropic.com)) classifies a higher share of recent posts as "hopelessness" rather than "anxiety" or "anger."

## What's driving the number this week

Three signals stood out:

- **CFPB complaint trajectory** — [8,500+ student loan complaints](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/consumer-complaints/) in the rolling 90-day window. That's not a record, but the *acceleration* is — +18% week-over-week.

- **Refinance search demand** — [Google Trends data](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?q=refinance%20student%20loans) shows a 24% spike in "refinance student loans" queries since Monday. Historically, that demand spike precedes a measurable shift in lender pipelines within 14 days.

- **Reddit theme drift** — The dominant topic in [r/StudentLoans](https://www.reddit.com/r/StudentLoans/) shifted from "income-driven repayment confusion" to "I missed a payment, what now?" That's a leading indicator for the next delinquency print.
