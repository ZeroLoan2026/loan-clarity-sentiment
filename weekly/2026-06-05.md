## What we said. What happened.

[Last week](https://www.studentloansindex.com/weekly/2026-05-29?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=issue-002-callback) we had three things on the watchlist. Here's the scorecard:

**1. CFPB Tuesday drop → ✓ confirmed.** 9,640 complaints in the trailing 90 days. +14% from the previous release. Highest single print since the October 2025 repayment restart. We called for acceleration; the data delivered. Slightly under our 18% estimate, but directionally a clean hit.

**2. SAVE plan court ruling → ✓ confirmed.** Narrow ruling, leaves the plan in limbo for at least another quarter. Reddit signal spiked 18% within 48 hours of the ruling landing — borrowers are reading the news.

**3. Nelnet earnings → ✗ surprised us.** Management was *more* cautious than we expected, with specific language about "elevated dispersion in 90-day delinquency across servicing portfolios." That phrase is going to get repeated on every [SLABS investor call](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=nelnet) for the next two quarters.

Two for three on the predictions. We'll take it.

## 📊 What's actually moving the number

Index up 2 points to 74. The underlying mix is more interesting than the headline:

**CFPB trajectory matters more than CFPB level.** We're running 18% above the trailing 12-week average. Sustained 15%+ acceleration historically front-runs delinquency prints by 60-90 days. Translation: if you model SLABS performance, the data your model needs hasn't been published yet — but we can see it forming.

**Reddit moved from "I missed a payment" to "what happens when wages get garnished."** Real shift in emotional register. Anthropic Claude classifies this as **anticipatory distress** — borrowers preparing for a hit they think is coming. Small share of posts (~6%), but the fastest-growing in our [methodology](https://www.studentloansindex.com/methodology?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter#ai-classification).

**Refi search cooled (-4% WoW).** Counter-intuitive. Anxiety usually pulls refi up. The cooling suggests borrowers are hitting the realization that federal loans can't be refinanced out of. That realization is its own leading indicator — for default risk.
