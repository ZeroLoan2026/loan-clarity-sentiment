## What we said. What happened.

[Last week](https://www.studentloansindex.com/weekly/2026-05-29?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=issue-002-callback) we flagged three things to watch. Two of them landed exactly as we expected. One surprised us.

**1. The Tuesday [CFPB complaint release](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/consumer-complaints/) — ✓ confirmed.** The drop showed 9,640 student loan complaints in the trailing 90 days, up 14% from the previous release and the highest single print since the repayment restart in October 2025. We were watching for confirmation that the prior week's spike was real, not an artifact. It's real.

**2. [Department of Education guidance on SAVE](https://studentaid.gov/announcements-events/) — ✓ confirmed.** The court issued a narrower-than-expected ruling that effectively keeps the program in limbo for another quarter. Borrower-side response on Reddit was immediate — our [r/StudentLoans](https://www.reddit.com/r/StudentLoans/) signal spiked 18% within 48 hours of the ruling.

**3. [Nelnet earnings](https://www.nelnet.com/investor-relations) — ✗ surprised us.** Management commentary was *more* cautious than we expected, with specific language about "elevated dispersion in 90-day delinquency across servicing portfolios." That's the kind of phrasing that gets repeated in [SLABS investor calls](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=nelnet) for the next two quarters.

## What's driving the number this week

The composite is up 2 points, but the underlying mix is more interesting:

- **CFPB complaint trajectory** — Now running 18% above the trailing 12-week average. Acceleration matters more than absolute level here — historically a sustained 15%+ acceleration has preceded delinquency prints by 60-90 days.

- **Reddit theme drift** — The dominant topic in [r/StudentLoans](https://www.reddit.com/r/StudentLoans/) shifted again this week: from "I missed a payment, what now?" to "what happens when wages get garnished." That's a meaningful escalation in the emotional register — Anthropic Claude classifies this as "anticipatory distress" in our [methodology](https://www.studentloansindex.com/methodology?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter#ai-classification).

- **Refinance search demand** — [Google Trends data](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?q=refinance%20student%20loans) cooled slightly (-4% WoW) after last week's spike. Interesting because rising anxiety usually pulls refi searches *up*. The cooling suggests borrowers may be hitting the realization that they can't refi out of federal loans — which is itself a leading indicator for default risk.

## Borrower voice

From [r/StudentLoans](https://www.reddit.com/r/StudentLoans/) this week — a post that captured what changed:

> "Wage garnishment notice today. $480 a month. I'm a teacher. I make $58K. I have a 4-month-old. I don't know what to tell my wife when I get home."
>
> <cite>— r/StudentLoans, June 2026 (anonymized)</cite>

This post got 2,400+ upvotes and 800+ comments in under 18 hours. A year ago, this kind of post would have gotten 50 upvotes and a few sympathetic replies. The change in collective response — comments are now overwhelmingly *"same here, same here, same here"* — is the part our [AI sentiment engine](https://www.studentloansindex.com/methodology?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter#ai-classification) flags as **shared crisis pattern.**

We've only seen this pattern twice in two years of tracking: March 2025 (just before the repayment restart announcement) and now.

## Watchlist — next week

Three things to watch closely:

- **[NY Fed Household Debt Report](https://www.newyorkfed.org/microeconomics/hhdc)** — drops Wednesday. Quarterly delinquency print is the canonical print for student loan stress. If 90+ day delinquency crosses 6.0% (was 5.7% last quarter), expect rating agency commentary.

- **[Sallie Mae earnings](https://www.salliemae.com/investors/)** — Tuesday after close. Watch for commentary on origination demand. If refi demand is softening (matching our Google signal), that's a tell.

- **A second SAVE plan court hearing** — scheduled Thursday. Could push borrowers from "limbo" to "definitely paying" within 60 days. The market hasn't priced this in.
