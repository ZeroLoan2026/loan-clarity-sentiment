## What we said. What happened.

[For weeks](https://www.studentloansindex.com/weekly/2026-06-19?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=issue-005-callback) we called July 1 a "coiled spring," not a turning point. The official confirmations this week settled it:

**1. SAVE exit process → ✓ confirmed, on the calendar.** The Department of Education [announced the next steps](https://www.ed.gov/about/news/press-release/us-department-of-education-announces-next-steps-borrowers-enrolled-unlawful-save-plan): starting July 1, servicers notify SAVE enrollees to move to a legal plan within **90 days**, or be auto-enrolled into the Standard or new Tiered Standard plan. This is the single largest forced repayment-plan migration in the program's history.

**2. New plans go live → ✓ as scheduled.** Two new options arrive July 1: the **Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP)** — payments scaled to income and dependents — and the **Tiered Standard Plan** with fixed 10/15/20/25-year terms by balance ([NPR's guide](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/10/nx-s1-5835633/student-loans-guide-education-changes-repayment-plan)). For many former SAVE borrowers who held a $0 payment, the new floor is higher.

**3. The delinquency print → ✗ worse than our model carried.** The [NY Fed's latest read](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20260512) puts 90+ day student-loan delinquency at **10.3%**, up from 9.6% the prior quarter — the highest since 2020. Our composite was tracking stress correctly in direction, but the realized data is at the severe end of the range.

We called the run-up. The data delivered it.

## 📊 What's actually moving the number

Index at 71, essentially flat on the week — but "flat at High Anxiety" five days before a forced migration is the story, not the stability. The macro underneath:

**Default is no longer a risk — it's a population.** Roughly [**9 million borrowers** — about 1 in 5 — are now in default](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/05/federal-student-loan-defaults-return-after-pandemic-pause/), the largest number on record, with 1 in 4 behind on payments. This is the exposed base walking into July 1.

**The credit damage is already booked.** Borrowers who fell into [default saw scores drop ~91 points](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/05/federal-student-loan-defaults-return-after-pandemic-pause/) (from 567 to 476); delinquent borrowers lost ~57. That's millions of consumers repriced out of auto, card, and mortgage credit — the spillover our [survey signal](https://www.studentloansindex.com/methodology?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=issue-005-sig-survey#emotions) keeps flagging.

**The trigger that hasn't fired yet: collections.** Involuntary collections remain suspended with [no firm restart date](https://www.npr.org/2026/02/10/nx-s1-5690186/student-loan-default-repayment) — but the mechanism (wage, Social Security, and tax-refund garnishment) is loaded. The day a restart date is announced is the day this index moves hard.

## 💬 What borrowers are saying this week

The register on [r/StudentLoans](https://www.reddit.com/r/StudentLoans/) has shifted from "is anything changing?" to "which plan do I pick, and what will it cost me?" Real, specific, transactional anxiety — borrowers comparing RAP estimates against the old SAVE $0, and bracing for a first bill they haven't had in years.

Representative of the week: people who qualified for SAVE *because* their income was low, now modeling a monthly payment for the first time and asking whether they can cover it. Our AI engine tags this as **payment-shock onset** — the phase where abstract policy becomes a number on a statement. Historically it precedes a delinquency uptick by 30–60 days.
