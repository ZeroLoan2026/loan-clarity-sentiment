## What we said. What happened.

[Last week](https://www.studentloansindex.com/weekly/2026-06-05?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=issue-003-callback) we put three items on the watchlist. The scorecard is the reason the number moved:

**1. NY Fed delinquency print → ✓ called the level, missed the break.** 90+ day delinquency came in at **5.7%** — flat, not the 6.0% break we flagged as the rating-agency trigger. The trigger didn't fire. That single non-event is the biggest reason the index cooled: our delinquency signal stopped climbing for the first time in two months.

**2. Sallie Mae / refi demand → ✗ the opposite happened.** We expected confirmation of last week's refi *cooling*. Instead [refinance search](https://www.studentloansindex.com/methodology?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=issue-003-sig-refi#refinance) re-accelerated hard — **+68% surge** on our tracker. Borrowers aren't giving up; they're shopping. That's the one signal moving *against* the cool-down, and we're not ignoring it.

**3. Second SAVE plan hearing → ✗ slipped.** The hearing got pushed. No ruling, no new shock — which removed an acute stressor the market had half-priced. Limbo extended is, paradoxically, calming in the short run.

Two of three drivers pointed down. The index did what the data told it to.

## 📊 What's actually moving the number

Down 17 points to 57. A drop this size is rare in our series, so it's worth being precise about *why* — and why we're not calling all-clear:

**Delinquency stalled, it didn't reverse.** Holding at [5.7%](https://www.studentloansindex.com/methodology?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=issue-003-sig-delinquency#delinquency) takes the acceleration out of the model, but the *level* is still elevated versus any pre-2025 quarter. The index reads "Neutral" on momentum, not on health.

**The refi re-surge is a tell, not a relief.** A [+68% refi-search spike](https://www.studentloansindex.com/methodology?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=issue-003-sig-refi#refinance) into a falling index is the kind of divergence that usually resolves toward stress, not away from it. Borrowers searching to refinance federal loans they *can't* refinance is the same trapped-demand pattern we flagged in Issue #2.

**Sentiment is still 80% worried — the floor under the number.** Our [survey signal](https://www.studentloansindex.com/methodology?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=issue-003-sig-survey#emotions) barely moved. The composite cooled because *momentum* cooled, not because borrowers feel better. With record [42M in IDR enrollment](https://www.studentloansindex.com/?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=issue-003-idr), the exposed population has never been larger heading into a rule change.

## 💬 What borrowers are saying this week

The dominant theme on [r/StudentLoans](https://www.reddit.com/r/StudentLoans/) shifted register again — from the garnishment dread that defined late May to something quieter: *"so is anything actually changing July 1 or not?"* Confusion, not panic.

Representative of the week's sentiment: borrowers describing a strange limbo — bracing for a hit that keeps getting rescheduled, unsure whether to refinance, pay ahead, or wait. Our AI engine tags this cluster as **suspended anxiety** — high baseline worry with the acute trigger deferred. It's exactly the pattern that snaps back fast when a hard date finally lands.

That hard date is in 19 days.
