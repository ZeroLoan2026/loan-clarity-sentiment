## What we said. What happened.

[Last week](https://www.studentloansindex.com/weekly/2026-06-12?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=issue-004-callback) we put three items on the watchlist and made one explicit call: the cool-down was a head-fake. Here's the scorecard:

**1. CFPB weekly complaint release → ✓ the relief was noise.** Complaints re-accelerated after the one-week pause — exactly the test we flagged. The single-week dip in our delinquency-driven momentum reversed, and the composite climbed straight back into High Anxiety. The cool-down didn't survive contact with the next data point.

**2. Refi conversion vs. trapped demand → ✓ trapped demand.** [Refi search](https://www.studentloansindex.com/methodology?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=issue-004-sig-refi#refinance) held its **+68%** elevation but did not convert — borrowers keep hitting the wall that federal loans can't be refinanced out of. Demand with nowhere to go is still our cleanest leading indicator for default risk.

**3. July 1 implementation guidance → ◐ trickling out.** Early servicer guidance started surfacing, but it's fragmented and inconsistent — which, if anything, *adds* to the confusion signal rather than resolving it. We're watching for the first clear, borrower-facing instruction set; it hasn't landed yet.

The contrarian call held. Don't-exhale was the right read.

## 📊 What's actually moving the number

Up 15 points to 72. A round-trip like this in seven days is unusual — here's the anatomy:

**The delinquency stall was temporary, not structural.** Holding at [5.7%](https://www.studentloansindex.com/methodology?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=issue-004-sig-delinquency#delinquency) cooled momentum for exactly one print. The moment the next CFPB data came in hotter, the [composite](https://www.studentloansindex.com/methodology?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=issue-004-sig-cfpb#cfpb) reverted hard. The level never improved; only the momentum paused.

**The refi divergence resolved toward stress — as expected.** We flagged the [+68% refi spike](https://www.studentloansindex.com/methodology?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=issue-004-sig-refi#refinance) into a falling index as a tell, not a relief. It resolved the way trapped-demand divergences usually do: the index caught back up to the stress the search behavior was already showing.

**Sentiment never left the danger zone.** Still [80% worried](https://www.studentloansindex.com/methodology?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=issue-004-sig-survey#emotions), with record [42M in IDR enrollment](https://www.studentloansindex.com/?utm_source=sentiment-friday&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=issue-004-idr). The "Neutral" week was always a momentum artifact sitting on top of an unchanged, historically large exposed population. Twelve days from a rule change, that floor is the whole story.
